Three ways to drive the development lifecycle as a circuit

All three use the same ten stops, from deciding what to build to learning from a mistake, and the lap starts again. At each stop the page says what the harness does there. At CI and deploy it does nothing, and the page says so. Scroll inside A and B; click rows in C.

A · Track mapB · The road goes onC · Timing board
A

Track map

A top-down circuit stays pinned on the left like a race track map, with the ten stops as corners. As you scroll the stops on the right, the car drives to that corner and the map names it.

Best for seeing the whole loop at once while reading one stop. Risk on phones the map has to shrink to a strip above the text.

B

The road goes on

The road from the game keeps going down the page. The car follows it as you scroll, and each bend has a pit board for its stop. Stops where the harness does nothing are plain road signs. At the bottom the lap starts again.

Best for continuity with the game; it feels like the same drive. Risk the longest section, and the winding road takes width on phones.

scroll inside this framethe car follows your scroll
C

Timing board

A compact circuit split into three sectors like a race timing screen: before code, while coding, after the push. The car laps on its own. The board lists every stop; open a row to see what the harness does and the car pulls in there.

Best for a short page and scanning: the whole lifecycle fits in one screen. Risk less of a story; it reads more like a spec.
